Your fighter carries two heat seekers, and the enemy fires the R-73. A heat seeker sees aircraft and flares, nothing else. **Every aircraft in the game gives off exactly the same heat**, whatever its throttle or speed. What the seeker gets depends on the angle it views that heat from.

## Powering and aiming the seeker

The rail seeker only works while heat seekers are the selected weapon (<Key k="B" /> switches). It is then **powered** in either of two ways:

- **Uncaged** with <Key k="R" />. The head searches along your nose and takes the enemy aircraft closest to it.
- **Slaved** to your radar's target, soft or hard lock. The head swings towards the radar's estimate of the target, as far as its gimbal allows.

**Firing cages the seeker again**, so uncage it again for the next shot.

On the HUD, a small circle shows where the head is looking. The seeker tone tells you what it has:

| Seeker state | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Searching | a growl that rises as an aircraft nears the head's line of sight |
| Designated, not locked | the growl, a little higher |
| Locked | a steady clean tone |

## Lock before and after launch

To pick an aircraft at all, the seeker looks inside a **designation cone** around your nose: the whole sphere for rounds that can fire over the shoulder, the cue angle for the R-73, R-73M and RVV-MD, and the gimbal for everything else. The seeker has an **infrared lock** when it can see the aircraft (see below) inside its gimbal.

- **Lock-before-launch rounds** need that infrared lock to fire. Without it the launch is refused with "Needs an infrared lock".
- **Lock-after-launch rounds** only need a designated aircraft, and only one inside the round's lock range from astern (see Range below). They leave the rail flying towards where it will be and lock on the way.

The lock-after-launch rounds are:

- **Over the shoulder, whole sphere:** AIM-9X Block II, AIM-9X Block II+, IRIS-T, ASRAAM, MICA IR and Python 5.
- **Inside their cue angle:** R-73, R-73M (45°) and RVV-MD (60°).
- **Inside their gimbal:** PL-10, AAM-5 and A-Darter.

## Holding the lock in flight

In flight the head can never look further from the missile's body than its **gimbal**. Four old rounds also have a narrow **field of view** and a slow head: the AIM-9B, AIM-9D, AIM-9H and R-3S. A slow head, like the AIM-9B's 11°/s, can fall behind a hard-turning target and lose it. Every other round holds its lock anywhere inside its gimbal.

[The missile catalog](/weapons/catalog/) lists each round's gimbal and seeker.

## Heat and aspect

The seeker reads the aircraft's heat times an **aspect factor** that depends on the seeker type:

| Seeker aspect | What it sees |
| --- | --- |
| Rear | Only from behind. **Nothing at or ahead of the target's beam.** |
| Rear cone (Magic I) | Only within 70° of the tail. |
| All-aspect | From every angle, but head-on only a fifth as bright as from dead astern. |
| Afterburner nose gate (AIM-9D only) | Rear, **four times brighter** when the target is in afterburner, and head-on within 20° of its nose while the afterburner is lit. |

**Afterburner only matters to the AIM-9D.** No other seeker reads it.

**Range.** Every seeker has a **lock range** against a fighter seen from dead astern, from 6 km for the rear-aspect rounds to about 22 km for the RVV-MD; [the missile catalog](/weapons/catalog/) lists them. It shrinks as the aspect moves forward: nose-on, an all-aspect head locks at under half of it. Beyond it the seeker sees nothing, even slaved to a radar lock. Whether a round can *reach* that far is down to its motor.

**What it ignores.** A heat seeker has **no line-of-sight check against the terrain**: a hill between it and the target does not break its lock. There is no sun and no ground heat either.

## Flares

A flare takes the seeker only if it is **at least twice as bright as the aircraft** from where the missile sits, inside the seeker's view, and not thrown out by the seeker's flare rejection. There is no random roll. While it follows a flare, the missile steers at the flare; once the flare fades below twice the aircraft's brightness, the seeker goes back to the aircraft if it can still see it.

| Flare rejection | Throws out a flare that is |
| --- | --- |
| None | never thrown out |
| Imaging | more than **1°** from the aircraft<SimChoice /> |
| Kinematic | slower than 85 % of the aircraft's speed and more than 2° from it<SimChoice /> |
| Rise-rate | brightening suddenly, the way a fresh flare does<SimChoice /> |

The flare has no aspect factor but the aircraft does, so a flare out-shines a target seen head-on for much longer than one seen from behind. [Flares](/survival/flares/) covers releasing them.

## Enemy heat seekers

The enemy always fires the **R-73**: all-aspect, lock after launch, 75° gimbal, 40 g, and no flare rejection. [Enemy pilots](/world/enemy-pilots/) lists when it fires.

<Callout kind="tip" title="In the cockpit">
  - **A rear-aspect round sees nothing ahead of the target's beam.** If the target turns its nose to the missile, the lock drops. The round flies 3 s of memory, then goes ballistic unless it locks again.
  - Lock with the radar (<Key k="T" />) and the seeker slaves to that target.
  - Against a target dropping flares, imaging seekers (AIM-9X, IRIS-T, ASRAAM, PL-10…) are the hardest to decoy.
</Callout>
