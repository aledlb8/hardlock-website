Each radar searches **its whole coverage**: ±60° either side of the nose on most jets, ±67° on the MiG-29, ±70° on the Typhoon, ±100° on the Gripen E and ±120° on the Su-35S. The beam paints that area one beam position at a time, row after row, always left to right. One full pass is a **frame**. The same coverage limits the track-while-scan box, the search tilt and how far a hard lock can follow, and the scope draws it. What you do with <Key k="T" />, <Key k="L" /> and <Key k="K" /> changes where it looks.

## Scan areas by mode

| Mode | Width | Height | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Search (SRCH) | the radar's coverage | about 16° (±8°) | Held on the horizon whatever your bank or pitch; tilt it with <Key k="↑" /> <Key k="↓" /> |
| Track-while-scan (TWS) on a mechanical radar | ±25° | 3 rows | Centred on your target, kept inside the coverage |
| Hard lock (STT) | the beam is held on the target | | |
| Dogfight (ACM) | ±15° | 20° tall, centred 6° above the nose | Fixed to the airframe, not levelled |

With a target selected, the scan's centre follows it up or down, as far as the tilt reaches. An electronic radar **never narrows to the ±25° box**: it keeps searching its whole coverage while it holds soft locks.

## Antenna tilt

Search stays on the horizon when you climb, dive or bank, so it keeps finding aircraft near your own height. It reaches about 8° above and below: about 2.8 km above and below you at 20 km, 5.6 km at 40 km, but only 1.4 km at 10 km. To look for aircraft well above or below you, hold <Key k="↑" /> or <Key k="↓" />. The search tilts 10° a second, up to about 52° either way (51° on the Gripen E and Su-57), as far as its top or bottom row stays inside the coverage. Two radars look less far down: the MiG-29's tilts 30° down and the Su-27S's 32°. The [elevation strip](/radar/reading-the-scope/#the-elevation-strip) and the EL figure on the scope show the tilt, and every match starts level. Once you select a target, the scan centres on it instead.

## The four modes

**Search** (SRCH): the full raster. Every confirmed contact shows on the scope.

**Soft lock, track-while-scan** (<Key k="T" />, TWS): the contact gets a number and becomes your target, and the scan goes on. A mechanical radar narrows to the ±25° box around the target. An electronic radar keeps searching its whole coverage and gives each numbered track an extra look about every 0.25 s.

**Hard lock, single-target track** (<Key k="L" />, STT): the beam is held on the target's predicted position. A hard lock can keep a target that search drops; see [Doppler, clutter and the notch](/radar/doppler-and-clutter/).

**Dogfight mode** (<Key k="K" />, ACM): the small box up the HUD. The radar hard-locks the nearest contact inside the box within 10 nm by itself, **on the first look**: the beam that paints it holds on it, and the lock confirms within a tenth of a second.

[Track numbers and locks](/radar/locking/) walks through the keys step by step.

## Mechanical and electronic radars

The scope names the antenna: MSA is a mechanically steered dish, PESA and AESA are electronically steered.

| | Mechanical (MSA) | Electronic (PESA, AESA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft lock | Narrows to a ±25° box, 3 rows | Keeps its whole coverage, plus a look at each numbered track every 0.25 s |
| Hard lock | Beam on the target, **search stops** | Target every other look, **search goes on at half speed** |
| Other tracks during a hard lock | Not revisited: they coast, then drop | Still revisited by the search |
| Ground clutter let in | Most | Less (PESA), least (AESA) |

### How far a lock can follow

A hard lock follows its target out to the edge of the radar's coverage, the same limits the search covers, across and up and down. Past that limit the beam goes back to scanning and the track coasts.

## Scope range

<Key k="Y" /> steps the scope through **10, 20, 40 and 80 km**, then wraps.

- When a new target is locked, the scope jumps to the smallest scale that fits it. That can zoom in as well as out.
- Entering dogfight mode sets 20 km.
- **A contact beyond the current scale is not drawn at all.** The radar still tracks it, so step the range out if the scope looks empty.

The scale only changes the display: the radar itself has no range setting.

## Hostile radar on or off

<Key k="N" /> turns the enemy aircraft's radars on or off. It is on by default and the setting is saved. The same switch is on the briefing and tactical screens. With it off, enemy radars stop scanning, your warning receiver hears no enemy radar, and enemy pilots fire no radar missiles. Your own radar is not affected. [Enemy pilots](/world/enemy-pilots/) covers how the AI then finds you.

## How enemy radars scan

Each enemy aircraft carries its own airframe's radar from the same catalog. It searches its set's whole coverage, tilted toward where its pilot believes you are. While it works a contact it uses the ±25°, 3-row box, **even on an electronic set**.

<Callout kind="tip">
  A wide search takes 3 to 5 s to come back to the same spot. On a mechanical radar a soft lock's ±25° box comes round in under a second, so the target is revisited four to six times as often. An electronic radar keeps the wide picture and updates the target on top.
</Callout>
