Your radar has **no fixed detection range and no lock range**. Each time the beam passes over an aircraft, the game works out how strong the echo is and rolls a chance to detect it. Contacts appear and firm up gradually as you close, rather than popping in at a set distance.

## Range and the reference target

The echo falls with the **fourth power of range**: twice the range means a sixteenth of the signal. Each radar is set so that it sees its own reference target (head-on, clear of the ground, in the middle of the beam) with a **50 % chance on one pass** at its reference range. Around that range the chance falls off quickly:

| Range compared with the reference range | half | 0.7× | 0.8× | 1× | 1.2× | 1.5× |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chance of detection per pass | 96 % | 84 % | 74 % | 50 % | 26 % | 8 % |

A target sitting between two beam positions, or seen at the edge of the beam, returns less than one in the middle of it.

## Cross section by aspect

What the beam sees is the target's own cross section at the angle it shows you, plus whatever it carries. Aspect comes from the aircraft's **nose and wings, never its flight path**, so a jet pulling hard toward or away from you shows you its top or belly.

Values in m². Nose figures are published (mostly analyst and press estimates), except the Gripen's, which is a game choice. The other aspects are worked out from the nose, and how they blend between angles is a sim choice.

| F-16C Block 50 | 1.2 | 100 | 3.2 | 710 | 480 |
| F-15C | 10 | 200 | 6.3 | 1420 | 960 |
| F/A-18E | 0.1 | 60 | 1.9 | 426 | 288 |
| F-22A | 0.0001 | 0.026 | 0.001 | 1.2 | 0.72 |
| F-35A | 0.0015 | 0.39 | 0.042 | 18 | 11 |
| JAS 39E Gripen | 0.1 | 40 | 1.3 | 284 | 192 |
| Typhoon | 0.1 | 60 | 1.9 | 426 | 288 |
| Rafale C | 0.1 | 60 | 1.9 | 426 | 288 |
| MiG-29 9.13 | 3 | 60 | 1.9 | 426 | 288 |
| Su-27S | 10 | 200 | 6.3 | 1420 | 960 |
| Su-35S | 3 | 200 | 6.3 | 1420 | 960 |
| Su-57 | 0.1 | 21 | 0.54 | 65 | 40 |
| J-20A | 0.05 | 2.4 | 0.26 | 115 | 68 |

The side, top and belly values are strong only near square-on: for the F-16 the return is about 1.9 m² at 45° off the nose and 39 m² at 70°.

**Missiles and rails hung outside add to the return**, and an empty rail still counts. The F-22A, Su-57 and J-20A carry everything inside and add nothing; the F-35A adds only its two heat seekers and their pylons.

The echo also changes from pass to pass, so the same aircraft at the same range can be bright on one sweep and faint on the next.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Most fighters are small nose-on and far bigger broadside or showing their belly. Turning side-on does not hide you from a radar: it makes you brighter. What can hide you is your speed along the beam, covered in [Doppler, clutter and the notch](/radar/doppler-and-clutter/).
</Callout>

## Terrain masking

**If terrain blocks the straight line between you and the target, there is no echo at all**, whatever the range. Warning receivers obey the same rule: a radar behind a ridge is not heard. See [Warnings](/survival/warnings/).

## What the radar can see

Your radar sees every aircraft not on your team. Your own side never appears on the scope, and missiles are not radar targets at all. Chaff can add interference to a jet's echo, but it never becomes a contact of its own on your scope.
