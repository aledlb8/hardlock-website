Your fighter has two warning receivers:

- **The RWR** hears enemy radars.
- **The missile-approach warner (MAWS)** sees missiles closing on you from behind.

They only report what they pick up. A heat seeker fired at you from the front sets off nothing.

## What the RWR hears

- **Enemy fighter radars**, but only while their beam is actually on you, not whenever you are inside their scan area.
- **Radar-missile seekers.** An enemy radar missile with its seeker switched on shows up as its own warning, even one chasing somebody else.

Infrared is never an RWR hit, including a heat seeker locked on you. When **Hostile radar** is off (<Key k="N" />), the enemy radars are silent.

## Search, soft lock, hard lock, launch

The receiver never reads what mode an enemy radar is in. It times **how often the beam lands on you, and for how long**. All the thresholds are sim choices.<SimChoice />

| Reading | What the beam does |
| --- | --- |
| Search | Sweeps over you. Lasts only while the beam is on you. |
| Soft lock | Keeps coming back: 3 separate passes within 3.5 s. |
| Hard lock | Stays on you for at least 40 % of the last 0.5 s (about 0.2 s of steady beam). Holds for 1 s after the beam lets go. |
| Launch | A hard lock whose radar is guiding one of its missiles off that track. |

A missile guided off a soft lock gives no launch warning.

<Callout kind="threat" title="The usual order">
  An enemy pilot follows you as a soft lock. When it wants to shoot, it asks for a hard lock and holds it for **1.5 s** before firing. So you normally hear soft lock, then hard lock, then launch. [Enemy pilots](/world/enemy-pilots/) covers when the AI shoots.
</Callout>

## The missile-approach warner covers your rear only

The MAWS warns about any missile you didn't fire when all of these are true:

- it is within **8 km**;
- it is within **about 57°** either side of your tail;
- terrain is not in the way;
- it is flying towards you.

It doesn't care whether the missile is a heat seeker or a radar missile. The enemy jets get a better warner than you: it covers all round, out to 10 km.<SimChoice />

<Callout kind="threat" title="Frontal heat-seeker shots are silent">
  A heat seeker is not a radar, so the RWR never hears it, and from ahead the approach warner doesn't see it either. **An R-73 fired at you from ahead gives no warning at all.** A radar missile from ahead shows up as LAUNCH (if the shooter holds a hard lock) and later as the seeker warning.
</Callout>

## Reading the RWR scope

The scope sits at the bottom right of the HUD. Your jet is in the middle, nose up, and a symbol's angle around the ring is its bearing. The more urgent a threat, the closer to the centre it sits:

| Threat | Symbol | Status text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search | F on the outer ring | SEARCH |
| Soft lock | F in a dashed ring | SOFT LOCK |
| Hard lock | F in a solid ring | HARD LOCK (steady box) |
| Launch | F in a double ring, with a line from the centre | LAUNCH (blinking) |
| Missile seeker | M in a diamond on the inner ring | MISSILE (blinking) |
| Missile approach | arrowhead on the inner ring, pointing in | MISSILE (blinking) |

- **Nothing heard** reads CLEAR.
- **Your chaff and flares** read CHF and FLR under the scope, and blink when empty.
- **Memory.** A symbol stays for **8 s** after it was last heard, fading. A lock you can no longer hear drops back to a search symbol, so the scope never claims a lock that has ended.

On the HUD itself, a large blinking **MISSILE** box appears under the heading tape whenever a missile seeker or an approach is being heard. It reads **LAUNCH** when a launch is the worst threat.

Every reading also has its own tone in your headset; [Sound](/interface/sound/#warning-tones) describes each one.

## Terrain blocks warnings

The RWR hears a radar only if terrain isn't in the way, and the approach warner needs the same clear line to the missile. Behind a ridge you won't hear a radar that can't see you anyway, but you also won't get an approach warning for a missile coming over the crest.

<Callout kind="tip" title="In the cockpit">
  Treat silence as "nothing heard", not "nothing coming". A hard lock is the step before a launch. When LAUNCH or MISSILE appears, the line on the RWR points at the threat.
</Callout>
