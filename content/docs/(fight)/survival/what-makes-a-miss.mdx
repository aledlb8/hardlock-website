The enemy fires two kinds of missile at you: the **R-73 heat seeker** and the game's **reference radar missile**. Each one is beaten by different things.

## Against the R-73 heat seeker

| What works | The number | More |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A flare at least twice as bright as you, inside its 75° gimbal | 2× brightness | [Flares](/survival/flares/) |
| Keeping flares coming | a pair every 0.3 s; it comes back to you as each flare fades | [Flares](/survival/flares/) |
| Showing it your nose or side, not your tail | you read five times dimmer from ahead or abeam | [Flares](/survival/flares/) |
| Getting more than 75° off its nose | it loses lock, flies 3 s of memory, then has no guidance until it relocks | [Out-flying](/survival/out-flying-a-missile/) |
| Making it turn high and slow after its burn | 3.5 s burn; turning limited by the air and capped at 40 g | [Out-flying](/survival/out-flying-a-missile/) |
| Being inside its arming distance | unarmed for 300 m: a hit is a dud | [Fuzes](/weapons/fuze-and-hits/) |

What does **not** help against the R-73:

- **Throttle and afterburner.** Your heat is fixed whatever the engine is doing.
- **Chaff.** Its seeker only looks at flares.
- **Terrain.** Its seeker never checks for terrain in the way.

## Against the radar missile

| What works | The number | More |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Making the shooter's radar lose you | it only feeds the missile while its track on you is confirmed and fresh | [Notching](/survival/the-notch/) |
| Notching the enemy radar | about ±23 to ±26 m/s with ground behind you | [Notching](/survival/the-notch/) |
| Dropping under its search speed gate | 46.3 to 51.4 m/s along its line of sight | [Notching](/survival/the-notch/) |
| Notching the missile's own seeker | about ±20 m/s with ground behind you; 75 ms puts it in memory | [Notching](/survival/the-notch/) |
| Leaving the notch somewhere it is not looking | its search grows with time since it last saw you | [Notching](/survival/the-notch/) |
| Chaff while crossing its line of sight | the cloud stays inside its gates only while you are beaming | [Chaff](/survival/chaff/) |
| Getting outside its field of view | about 29° around its flight path | [Out-flying](/survival/out-flying-a-missile/) |
| Putting terrain between you | seeker and radar both need a clear line | [Notching](/survival/the-notch/) |
| Outlasting it | 120 s battery | [Out-flying](/survival/out-flying-a-missile/) |

What does **not** help against the radar missile:

- **Flares.** Radar seekers don't see them.
- **Turning it until it is slow.** Its turn limit doesn't fall off with height or speed, and turning costs it no speed.
- **Chaff on its own**, while you fly straight at it or away from it.

[Out-flying a missile](/survival/out-flying-a-missile/#the-miss-distance-that-counts) shows how close each one has to pass to hurt you.

## Read the warnings, but don't trust silence

- **A heat seeker from ahead gives no warning.** The RWR only hears radar, and the approach warner covers only your rear.
- **LAUNCH** means a radar has a hard lock on you and its missile is flying off that track.
- **The M diamond** means an enemy radar missile has its seeker on, not necessarily aimed at you.

[RWR and missile warnings](/survival/warnings/) has the details.

<Callout kind="tip" title="In the cockpit">
  For the R-73: flares early and often, show it your side rather than your tail, and make it turn up high after its motor stops. For the radar missile: put it on your beam with ground behind you, add chaff, and hold it until the missile has gone past you.
</Callout>
