**You cannot over-stress or stall the jet.** On the F-16 a fly-by-wire holds every limit. On the other jets the lift cap and the card's g limits do the same job. Nothing breaks if you pull hard, because the game has no structural damage from g. **The ground is what kills you.**

## The F-16's fly-by-wire limits

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Positive g | +9 g |
| Negative g | −3 g |
| Angle of attack | 25° |
| Roll rate | Up to 308 °/s, less at low speed and at high angle of attack, never below 80 °/s |
| Rudder | ±6° of sideslip, fading out at high angle of attack |
| Spin prevention | Above 29° angle of attack, roll commands are ignored |

Two limits govern every pull: 25° of angle of attack, and the angle that would make 9 g. **Whichever comes first wins.** Fast, you hit 9 g first. Slow, you hit 25° long before 9 g, and the pull just stops getting harder.

## Limits on the other jets

The point-mass jets fly their card's g limits; [the roster](/aircraft/roster/) marks which are estimates.

- **Lift cap:** the g available shrinks as you slow down, set by the wing area and the jet's maximum lift. That is how these jets run out of pull at low speed. They cannot stall or spin.
- **Angle of attack:** the HUD shows it climbing to the jet's maximum-lift angle, from 26° on the Gripen to 40° on the F/A-18E.<SimChoice />
- **Roll:** each jet's own rate, from 160 °/s on the F/A-18E to 250 °/s on the Typhoon and Rafale, cut to as little as 30 % of that at low speed.<SimChoice />

## Speed limits

**No jet takes damage from speed.** The F-16 has no airspeed limit in flight: its card shows a 1,555 km/h indicated-airspeed limit, but the flight model does not enforce it. The point-mass jets are capped at 1,300 m/s.<SimChoice />

## The ground

**Coming within 1.5 m of the terrain is a crash**, and water counts as terrain. The jet has no landing gear and no runway. The whole path between two steps is checked, so you cannot skip through a ridge.

The HUD's ALT row is height above your base's ground level, **not height above the ground under you**. The mountains reach about 2.6 km and higher. [The theater](/world/theater/) describes the terrain, and [Kills, crashes and rearming](/world/outcomes/) covers what happens after a crash.

## How turning costs speed

Pulling g costs speed on every jet, because making more lift makes more drag. The harder the pull, the faster it grows: on the point-mass jets the extra drag grows with the square of your g, and faster still as you near maximum lift. A full 9 g pull in the F-16 at 3 km on afterburner bleeds about 100 m/s in 6 s.

The point-mass jets top out at their card's sea-level speed down low and at their published top Mach high up: Mach 1.6 for the F/A-18E and F-35A, Mach 2.5 for the F-15C. Drag rises steeply through Mach 1, so **the last few tenths of Mach come slowly**.

## Corner speed

Corner speed here is the slowest speed at which a jet can reach its g limit. Below it, the lift limit binds first. This is the instantaneous figure, not a sustained turn. Worked out from each card's numbers:

| Jet | Sea level | 3 km |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F-16C (estimate) | about 186 m/s | about 216 m/s |
| F-15C | 162 m/s | 188 m/s |
| F/A-18E (+7.5 g) | 154 m/s | 179 m/s |
| F-22A | 155 m/s | 180 m/s |
| F-35A | 185 m/s | 214 m/s |
| JAS 39E Gripen | 170 m/s | 198 m/s |
| Eurofighter Typhoon | 152 m/s | 177 m/s |
| Rafale C | 151 m/s | 175 m/s |
| MiG-29 9.13 | 172 m/s | 199 m/s |
| Su-27S | 168 m/s | 195 m/s |
| Su-35S | 180 m/s | 209 m/s |
| Su-57 | 155 m/s | 180 m/s |
| J-20A | 173 m/s | 201 m/s |

<Callout kind="tip">
  - Hard pulls are paid for in speed, and slow jets cannot pull much.
  - Above corner speed you get full g but bleed fast.
  - Watch the ground, not just the ALT row: over the mountains, the terrain can be thousands of metres above the level the HUD measures from.
</Callout>
