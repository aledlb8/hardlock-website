**You cannot stall the jet, and you cannot over-g it by accident.** Every jet's fly-by-wire holds its limits. A few jets let you pull past the g limit on purpose with the [g override](#the-g-override-and-over-g), and that is the only way to bend or break one.

Two limits govern every pull: the angle-of-attack limit, and the angle that would make the g limit. **Whichever comes first wins.** Fast, you hit the g limit first. Slow, you hit the angle-of-attack limit long before it, and the pull just stops getting harder. On the F-22A and Su-35S the angle-of-attack limit sits past the angle where the wing makes the most lift: a full pull points the nose further, but the flight path turns no faster and the speed bleeds away.

- **Roll rate** is the most each jet will roll. It falls at low speed and at high angle of attack (on the F-16, never below 80 °/s).
- **During a hard push**, while the fly-by-wire holds the nose at its lowest angle of attack, it also slows the roll, so rolling does not drag the nose through that limit.
- **Rudder:** up to ±6° of sideslip, fading out as you near the angle-of-attack limit.
- **Spin prevention:** 4 to 5° past the angle-of-attack limit (29° on the F-16), roll and rudder commands are ignored and the surfaces only stop the jet yawing.

## The g override and over-g

Hold <Key k="L Alt" hold /> to pull past the g limit, on the jets that allow it:

| Jet | With the override held |
| --- | --- |
| F/A-18E | The limit rises to +10 g. |
| F-15C, MiG-29 9.13, Su-27S | No g ceiling. The jet pulls as hard as the wing can lift, which at speed is far more than the wing can take. The angle-of-attack limit still holds. |
| Every other jet | Nothing changes. The limit is hard. |

The hangar card's **G override** line shows the same: To +10.0 g, No g ceiling, or None.

**Past the limit, the airframe bends.** The HUD's G row blinks OVER-G while you are past it. Each pull costs structure by how far past the limit it peaks, not by how long you hold it, and the next pull costs again:

- **A small overshoot is free.** Up to 0.2 g past the limit bends nothing.
- **The wing fails at about 1.5 times the limit:** about 13 g on a fresh 9 g jet, and about 10.9 g on a fresh F/A-18E. Lost structure brings that closer, from missile blasts or from earlier over-g pulls. A jet with half its structure gone breaks halfway between its limit and 1.5 times it.
- **A full override pull to 10 g in the F/A-18E** costs about 6% of the structure. Five of them leave the wing just strong enough; the sixth breaks it.
- **When the wing fails**, the jet breaks up where it is flying and the HUD shows WING FAILED.

<Callout kind="threat">
  On the F-15C, MiG-29 9.13 and Su-27S, holding the override at high speed with the aim circle far off the nose reaches 13 g in a fraction of a second. Use it in a short, deliberate pull and let go.
</Callout>

## Speed limits

**No jet takes damage from speed, and no jet has an airspeed limit in flight.** Drag alone sets how fast each one goes.

## The ground

**Coming within 1.5 m of the terrain is a crash**, and water counts as terrain. The jet has no landing gear and no runway. The whole path between two steps is checked, so you cannot skip through a ridge.

The HUD's ALT row is height above your base's ground level, **not height above the ground under you**. The mountains reach about 2.6 km and higher. [The theater](/world/theater/) describes the terrain, and [Kills, crashes and rearming](/world/outcomes/) covers what happens after a crash.

## How turning costs speed

Pulling g costs speed on every jet, because making more lift makes more drag, and the harder the pull, the faster it grows. A full 9 g pull in the F-16 at 3 km on afterburner bleeds about 100 m/s in 6 s.

On full afterburner each jet tops out at about its card's sea-level speed down low and its top Mach at 11 km ([the roster](/aircraft/roster/) lists them). Drag rises steeply through Mach 1, so **the last few tenths of Mach come slowly**.

## Corner speed and turn rates

Corner speed here is the speed to enter a full pull at for the fastest turn it gives. Slower, the wing runs out of lift before the g limit; faster, the g limit holds the turn rate down. All figures are on full afterburner and rounded, so read them as "about":

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| F-15C | 140 m/s | 165 m/s | 28.6 °/s | 19.0 °/s at 240 m/s |
| F/A-18E | 140 m/s | 160 m/s | 24.2 °/s | 15.6 °/s at 242 m/s |
| F-22A | 130 m/s | 155 m/s | 34.5 °/s | 19.3 °/s at 242 m/s |
| F-35A | 140 m/s | 160 m/s | 30.0 °/s | 14.3 °/s at 293 m/s |
| JAS 39E Gripen | 175 m/s | 195 m/s | 25.7 °/s | 14.1 °/s at 268 m/s |
| Eurofighter Typhoon | 155 m/s | 175 m/s | 27.6 °/s | 17.5 °/s at 266 m/s |
| Rafale C | 140 m/s | 165 m/s | 30.6 °/s | 18.8 °/s at 270 m/s |

The fastest turn lasts only a moment: the pull bleeds speed at once. The sustained turn is the fastest level turn the jet can hold without slowing down.

<Callout kind="tip">
  - Hard pulls are paid for in speed, and slow jets cannot pull much.
  - Above corner speed you get full g but bleed fast.
  - Watch the ground, not just the ALT row: over the mountains, the terrain can be thousands of metres above the level the HUD measures from.
</Callout>
