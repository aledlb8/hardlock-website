**Every aircraft in the game is a full airframe**, with forces, moments, inertia, control surfaces that move at limited rates and an engine that spools. Each jet has its own wing, weight, engines and limits, so no two fly alike.

Whoever is flying, you or an enemy pilot, only says where the nose should go and how much thrust to use. The jet's fly-by-wire then moves the surfaces.

## What every airframe has

- **Forces and moments** follow the jet's angle of attack, sideslip, speed and control surfaces, so the way it responds changes with speed and with how hard you pull.
- **Real inertia**, and control surfaces that move at limited rates, so it takes a moment to start and stop a roll or a pull.
- **A fly-by-wire flies the surfaces for you** and holds each jet's g, angle-of-attack and roll limits. See [G, angle of attack and energy](/flying/limits-and-energy/).
- **An engine that spools**, with thrust that changes with altitude and Mach. See [Throttle, afterburner and fuel](/flying/engine-and-fuel/).
- **Drag that rises steeply through Mach 1.** On full afterburner each jet tops out near its card's sea-level speed down low and its top Mach at 11 km. [The roster](/aircraft/roster/) lists them.

The F-16's aerodynamics come from NASA TP-1538 (1979), a wind-tunnel and simulator study of the F-16, as printed in Stevens & Lewis, *Aircraft Control and Simulation*. The F-15C and F/A-18E fly public NASA research data carried to their own wings. The other jets' tables are built from their public size, weight and engines with textbook aerodynamics.

## Canards and thrust vectoring

The Gripen, Typhoon, Rafale and J-20A move their canard foreplanes with the pitch command.

Three jets vector their thrust: the **F-22A** in pitch (±20°), the **Su-35S** in pitch and yaw (±15°) and the **Su-57** in pitch and yaw (±16°). The nozzles follow the stick like an extra control surface. They turn the nose with engine thrust rather than airflow, so they keep working when the jet is slow.

<Callout kind="sim" title="The F-16 has no Mach effect on lift">
  The F-16's NASA tables are low-speed data, so its lift and handling are the same at every Mach; only its drag rises past Mach 1. On every other jet, lift and control power change with Mach and fall away above Mach 1.
</Callout>

## Enemy jets fly the same model

Enemy aircraft use the same flight models and the same mouse-aim style controls as you. Their pilot supplies an aim direction and a throttle. [Enemy pilots](/world/enemy-pilots/) covers how they decide where to point.
