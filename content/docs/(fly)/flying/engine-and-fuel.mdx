**There is no fuel in Hardlock's jets.** No fuel is burned and you cannot run dry. The throttle and the afterburner only decide how much thrust you get. The only weight that changes is your missiles: each round on board adds its own, and firing it makes the jet lighter. A heavy load can lower your [g limit](/flying/limits-and-energy/#when-the-g-limit-is-lower).

## The throttle

The throttle runs from 0 to 110 %. **100 % is full dry power, and anything above it is afterburner.**

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <Key k="Shift" hold /> | Opens the throttle at 50 % per second, so 0 to 100 % takes 2 s. |
| <Key k="Ctrl" hold /> | Closes it at the same rate. |
| <Key k="X" /> | Lights the afterburner by jumping the throttle to 110 %. Press it again to go back to where the throttle was before, at most 100 %. |

Every start and restart puts you at 85 %. The HUD's THR row shows the throttle as a percentage, with an inverted AB chip beside it while the afterburner is lit. **The afterburner is all or nothing**: anywhere past 100 % gives the same full afterburner.

## The engine

Every jet's engine follows the F-16's NASA engine data, scaled to its own ratings, so on every jet thrust falls with height and changes with Mach. [The roster](/aircraft/roster/) lists each jet's dry and afterburner thrust at a standstill at sea level. Every jet has an afterburner, and lighting it always adds thrust.

**Every engine spools.** From idle it takes about 4 s to reach military power and about 5 s to reach full afterburner. From military power the afterburner lights in under a second. **The Rafale C spools faster**: about 2.5 s from idle to military power and about 3 s to full afterburner.

At idle and high speed the engine pushes backwards, so chopping the throttle at speed slows you faster than drag alone would.

The F-16's fixed intake loses pressure through the shock in front of it above Mach 1, so its thrust falls away and it tops out near Mach 1.95 high up.

## Supercruise

Two jets fly well past Mach 1 without the afterburner: at 11 km the **F-22A** reaches **Mach 1.82** on dry power and the **Typhoon Mach 1.49**. Every other jet tops out near Mach 1 on dry power (the F-16 at Mach 1.12), and needs the afterburner to go faster.

## The afterburner and your heat signature

Your heat follows your engine. At full afterburner your tail reads about **four times** as bright as at military power, and the flame shows from the side too; at idle, once the tailpipe has cooled, about a quarter. The tailpipe holds its heat for a few seconds after you throttle back, so cancel the afterburner early. The HUD's HEAT figure shows it. [Heat seekers](/weapons/heat-seekers/#how-hot-a-jet-runs) explains what each seeker sees.

<Callout kind="tip">
  - Open the throttle before you need the speed: the engine takes seconds to spool up.
  - With an R-73 behind you, cancel the afterburner and throttle back early: the R-73 sees a full afterburner twice as far, and from behind no flare outshines it.
  - In the F-22A or the Typhoon, cruise fast on dry power and save the afterburner for the fight.
</Callout>
